Paper Smash Bros

I'm trying to make a fighting game (like Super Smash Bros) as a card game. Not sure why, but it seems like an interesting concept, so I'm going for it. The best part about it would be that the players can make new characters and abilities just by making paper cards.

Overview

On the table, there will be a grid that represents a side-view platform map (like in most fighting videogames). There will be game pieces on the board showing the location of each player. Each player will have a piece of paper they use to track their health, as well as a deck of cards representing the capabilities of the character they chose. Players take turns playing actions until there's only one character left on the map.

Setup

  1. Players each choose a character, taking the move deck for the character they chose, and drawing the health grid for their character.
  2. Players follow any additional setup instructions for their character (if any).
  3. Players begin taking turns.

On your first turn, you may place your game piece wherever you want on the map. You may not play any cards, but you may prepare for your next turn by arranging your deck however you wish and placing it face-down in front of you.

On your turn

In most games, you decide what to do and then do it. In this game, you decide what to do on your next turn, and then you do the thing you decided to do on your previous turn. Here's how it works:

Action Phase:

  1. Flip your deck face-up.
  2. Play the card that is now face-up, and discard it face-down next to your deck.
  3. During the process of playing the card, you may end up playing or skipping other cards. All these cards are discarded face-down in a pile on top of the first card without changing their order.
  4. Finally, if your game piece is not supported by a platform of some sort, or by another player, and if none of the cards you played specified that there's "no gravity" or "0G", move your piece two spaces down (because of gravity).

Planning Phase:

  1. Take the remaining cards in your deck and put them in any order you want.
  2. Place those cards face-down on the table, and stack the discarded cards on top of them.
  3. You may not rearrange your deck until your next turn.

Notice that any cards that are discarded during your turn cannot be placed at the bottom of the deck for your next turn. This prevents players from doing the same thing each turn (generally).

Card types

There are several types of cards:

  • Regular - Do something.
  • Free - Do something, then play another card.
  • A - Do something, then play the next card too if it's a B card (not optional).
  • B - Do something. Activated for free if it comes after an A card.
  • Trap - Do nothing. We'll get to this in a sec.
  • Stance - Place the card face-up in front of you. More on this later.

Examples of possible cards:

  • (Regular) Attack a player immediately above you with an L-shape of damage
  • (Regular) Skip a certain number of cards and then play another card
  • (A) Attack, followed by (B) Move
  • (Free) You may skip the next card (and play the card after it instead)
  • (Regular) Jump up 4 squares

Damage

Rather than simply keeping track of a number for health-points, each player will draw a grid on a piece of paper. Their character card specifies how big the grid should be, which squares on the grid need to start out empty, and which need to start out full. When damage is dealt, the card will show a picture of a shape made of squares. The player doing the damage will decide where to place the shape on the target player's grid (rotating and flipping the shape is fine) so that it covers as many empty spots as possible. All spots covered by the shape are then filled in. When the last empty square is filled, that player is K.O'd. Their game-piece is removed from the game board and they are done playing.

  • Some squares are marked with a shield. Damage shapes are not allowed to be placed on a shield. This can make it tricky to deal damage in certain cases. Since shield squares cannot be filled in, they don't count towards the total of empty squares.
  • Some squares are marked with a star. When a star square is filled in, the player who got damaged is stunned.

Stunning

When a player is stunned, their turn ends (if they're currently taking their turn), and their deck is placed face-up to indicate that they've been stunned. On their next turn, they don't get to play any cards (but they can still organize their deck so that they can play a card next turn).

Trap Cards

When someone is about to be damaged, they can flip their deck face-up to reveal a trap card (if they have a trap card on the bottom of their deck). The trap card determines what happens. Trap cards always get the final say about what happens. Making cards that say something like, "Do damage to the player even if they reveal a trap card" is not allowed.

Trap cards are useful for:

  • Blocking damage
  • Reflecting damage back to the player who's doing it
  • Gaining energy tokens (or something) according to the damage taken
  • Somehow punishing the player that dealt the damage (stun, end their turn, etc)
  • Whatever else you can think of

Stance Cards

Stance cards are placed face-up on the table in front of you, and they stay there until after you play another card. They exist to modify or qualify the next action you're about to take. Example:

  1. Turn 1: You play a stance card "Meditative Calm".
  2. Your enemy approaches, but it's too late for them. They see your stance and they know what's coming.
  3. Turn 2: You play "Chi Blast" (requires "Meditative Calm" stance), and then you take the stance card back into your deck.

Stance cards remain on the table until you play a card. Any time you play a card, you must reclaim your currently active stance card (if any). Thus, stance cards can only affect the next card you play.

Vocabulary

Here are some helpful words with standard meanings that are used in these rules and that can be used on the cards for concision:

  • Back of deck - The side of the deck that is touching the table when placed face-up.
  • Front of deck - The side of the deck that is facing up when the deck is placed face-up.
  • Play - Play a card
  • Reclaim - To take a card from wherever it is and place it at the back of your deck.

House Rules

It's not possible to specify every edge-case in advance–especially when I cannot predict what cards you'll invent. Therefore, it's a good idea for you to keep your own list of house rules. Whenever there's a disagreement, work together to come to a concensus, and record your decision in an ever-growing list of rulings. If you can predict in advance what might cause a disagreement, you should probably make a decision beforehand. This doubles as a useful way to reduce the number of words that need to be written on cards. By deciding beforehand how to handle various situations, you can let your house rules do more of the heavy lifting so that your cards don't have to specify everything themselves.

To help you make your house rules, I've compiled a list of principles to guide you:

  • Favor fun over realism.
  • Favor rules that make it easier and more intuitive to write cool cards with less words.
  • If a character is the problem, fix the character, not the rules.
